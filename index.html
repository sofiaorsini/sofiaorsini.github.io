01 · Automotive · Global car manufacturer · 2025–2026 · Interaction Design

Redesigning a dealership CRM around how dealers actually sell

Dealers were ignoring a global automaker's CRM, leaving most of its sales in Brazil untraceable. I led the redesign into a mobile-first app where tracking a sale is a byproduct of selling, now live across the brand's entire Brazilian dealership network.

1,900

Challenge

The automaker's dealership CRM was largely ignored by dealers, leaving most sales in Brazil untraceable and costing the brand valuable market data. The challenge: track every sale without forcing dealers into a process that doesn't fit how they sell.

Strategy

I started by investigating why the CRM's first version (1.0) failed. The key insight was that the system forced a fixed sales sequence that didn't match how salespeople work on the floor. I translated that into a redesigned, mobile-first experience built around efficiency and low-effort user flows, so that traceability became a byproduct of selling rather than extra work.

My role

  • Led interaction design for the new CRM mobile app
  • Designed the flexible sales flow that balances dealer freedom with data traceability
  • Rebuilt an existing guideline into an accessible, functional component library
  • Structured the design operation for a newly formed team

Dealers need a CRM that fits their experience on the floor

Ten interviews with pilot dealerships during discovery were the basis for defining design principles.

Research insightDesign principle

The system felt mechanical and the flow was too rigid. Selling is intuitive and relationship-based, but 1.0 treated it as a sequence of steps to complete.

Support the relationship, not the procedure.

Salespeople must be able to manage opportunities and capture data in a flexible manner.

Core tools were missing. 1.0 didn't help dealers turn a lead into a closed sale: no financing simulation, no used vehicle evaluation.

Integrate with existing tools.

Dealerships already use tools for these solutions, but having them all in one place could help them keep better track of sales opportunities over the long-term.

The pace is relentless. Online leads come with a short SLA while in-person customers need attention at the same time.

Build for speed.

The most urgent work is visible at a glance, and the most frequent actions take as few taps as possible.

Traceability as a byproduct of selling

Designing the opportunity pipeline so selling and tracking are the same action.

Users can register qualified sales actions (like test drives, financing simulations and used vehicle evaluations) in any order, or not register any at all. The only mandatory parts of the user flow are contacting the lead (for online-originated leads) and registering the result of each opportunity.

Growing adoption over 60 days since launch

The new CRM has been live across all of the brand's Brazilian dealerships since August 2026. Overall adoption figures still point to experience challenges, but present a positive trend. The automaker has been able to gain more visibility into the dealerships' process and bring more data into their ecosystem.

Challenge

A 60-person digital team at a global industrial leader was delivering what stakeholders asked for, with no process to validate whether it solved real user needs. The cost: effort and budget spent on solutions that might never be used.

Strategy

The research framework

From ad-hoc processes to a consistent, repeatable practice.

Giving the business visibility of our processes, activities and timeline was paramount in building a trusting relationship.

Deep dive: one trustworthy number, not a dashboard

Validating a high-level idea and refocusing development efforts.

The request

A business area asked for a real-time dashboard in the Customer Portal showing each customer's refractory consumption and the associated Scope 3 CO₂ emissions. Estimated effort: ~3 months of development, with open technical questions about keeping the data accurate and up to date.

Our approach

Instead of starting development, we ran a 2-week research sprint: we prototyped the requested dashboard, tested it with real customers, and interviewed them about what they actually needed from CO₂ transparency.

What we learned

Customers need one trustworthy number, not a dashboard. The Relative Emissions KPI (all refractories purchased for the plant over a year) is the one they report on and would use to compare suppliers. One customer called it the number "that really matters."

They want a yearly view and a future outlook. Monthly granularity was unnecessary for both interviewees, and customers cared more about where the company is heading than about detailed history.

Our impact

The solution shifted to a self-service feature in the Customer Portal: customers download a yearly emissions overview as a PDF or XLS, generated from existing data.

  • Lower development and maintenance cost
  • Less manual work for Technical Marketing
  • Room for more detail and explanatory text than a dashboard allows

03 · Innovation hub · Backed by a major bank · 2026 · Service Design and AI

Six weeks to rethink how an innovation hub serves its customers

A startup and innovation hub backed by a major bank needed to scale its hybrid value proposition without diluting the connections that make its ecosystem valuable. I ran the service design engagement end to end, with an AI-assisted research workflow, and presented the recommendations to the CEO.

21

interviews with internal teams and customers

3

17

Challenge

The hub needed to scale its value proposition in a hybrid (physical and digital) model without diluting the quality of the connections that make the ecosystem valuable. The project had six weeks, and our team would not take part in implementation, so the recommendations had to be concrete enough to stand on their own.

Strategy

I planned and ran the project end to end, from commercial proposal to final delivery. The as-is was mapped through 21 interviews with the hub's internal teams and customers, resulting in 4 detailed service blueprints. We then co-created the to-be in 3 workshops with the hub's leadership.

Results

Delivered 17 new initiatives, organized into structural actions, quick wins, experiments, and a platform feature backlog. I presented the strategic recommendations directly to the hub's CEO and managers, tied to two 2026 OKRs (customer satisfaction and retention, and business model sustainability). The final documentation was also delivered as a custom AI agent that the hub's team can keep feeding with new sources.

AI embedded into the research process and strategic thinking

Custom agents for planning, personas and analysis, with human approval at every stage of the research synthesis.

From planning to result consolidation

To ensure delivery within the project deadline, I built custom AI agents and skills to support the process:

  • Service Design Specialist, trained on materials from my own education, for activity planning and artifact creation
  • Synthetic personas for the hub's customer segments, for desk research and artifact refinement
  • UX Researcher, for qualitative data analysis from interview outputs
Reliability through structured process

The UX Researcher agent went through sequential analysis levels with human approval gates, to reduce the risk of hallucinations:

  1. Transcript
  2. Refined transcript
  3. Synthesis and thematic analysis per interview
  4. Synthesis and thematic analysis per persona
  5. Pattern identification
  6. General and persona-level insight consolidation
Handing over extensive documentation

The project's final documentation was also delivered as a customized agent in the client's Notion environment, making it easier to consume recommendations and enrich the information with new knowledge sources in the long term.
